Suppose two equations must hold at once, such as 2x + y = 5 and x − y = 1. This is a linear system. Writing it as a matrix lets you solve it step by step with a method that works for any number of equations.

The augmented matrix

Take only the coefficients and constants and put them in one matrix, separating the constants with a bar:

2x +  y = 5            [ 2   1 | 5 ]
 x −  y = 1     →      [ 1  −1 | 1 ]

Row operations

Three moves keep the solutions unchanged:

  • Swap two rows.
  • Multiply a row by a nonzero number.
  • Add a multiple of one row to another row.

Use them to build zeros below the diagonal. Adding the two rows above gives 3x = 6, so x = 2. Substituting back into x − y = 1 gives y = 1.

The geometry

Each linear equation in two unknowns is a line. The reference graph shows one such line, y = 2x + 1. A system asks where the lines meet, and there are three possibilities:

  • One solution: the lines cross at a single point.
  • No solution: the lines are parallel and never meet.
  • Infinitely many solutions: the equations describe the same line.
Key idea: solving a system means finding the point where all the lines meet, and row operations do this without guessing.
