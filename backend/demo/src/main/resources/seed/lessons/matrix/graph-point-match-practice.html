Solving equations often boils down to a small, familiar step: put a known value in and see what the unknown must be. This practice uses exactly that idea on a line.

The task

After inspecting the reference line, adjust the slope of y = mx + 1 so the graph passes through the target point (2, 5).

Reason it out instead of guessing

A point lies on the line when its coordinates satisfy the equation. Substitute x = 2 and y = 5:

5 = m × 2 + 1
4 = 2m
m = 2

So with m = 2 the line reaches the point (2, 5). When x = 2, the graph should produce y = 5.

What the slope tells you

  • The slope m is how much y rises for each step of 1 in x.
  • All these lines share the y-intercept 1, so they all pass through (0, 1). Changing m pivots the line around that point.

Connect to systems

Finding m so that a line passes through a point is a tiny linear system. Passing through (0, 1) and (2, 5) fixes a single line, exactly as two equations fix a single solution.

Key idea: substitute a known point into the equation, then solve for the one remaining unknown.
