Arrows are great for intuition, but to calculate we need numbers. Placing a vector on a coordinate grid turns it into a pair (or triple) of numbers we can add, scale, and measure.

Components

A vector in the plane can be described by how far it goes across (x) and how far it goes up (y). These are its components, written a = ⟨x, y⟩. The vector ⟨3, 4⟩ means “3 to the right, 4 up”.

Magnitude by Pythagoras

The two components form the legs of a right triangle, and the vector is the hypotenuse. So its length is:

|a| = √(x² + y²)

For ⟨3, 4⟩ we get √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5. This is the famous 3-4-5 triangle. Other whole-number triples that work are 5-12-13 and 8-15-17.

Direction

The angle θ that the vector makes with the positive x-axis satisfies tan θ = y / x. For ⟨3, 4⟩ this is about 53°.

Arithmetic with components

  • Add: ⟨a, b⟩ + ⟨c, d⟩ = ⟨a + c, b + d⟩
  • Scale: k⟨a, b⟩ = ⟨ka, kb⟩

This is much easier than tip-to-tail drawing, and it extends to three dimensions with a third component z.

Key idea: the magnitude of a vector is the length of the hypotenuse formed by its components.

Try it

Adjust x and y until the magnitude equals 5. There is more than one answer. Start with the 3-4-5 triangle and try both orders (3, 4) and (4, 3), and also the trivial case (5, 0).
