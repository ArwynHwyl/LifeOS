The dot product tells you how aligned two vectors are. The cross product answers a different question: given two vectors in space, what is a direction perpendicular to both, and how big is the parallelogram they span?

Definition

The cross product a × b is defined for vectors in three dimensions. The result is a vector (unlike the dot product). It has two important properties:

  • Direction: perpendicular to both a and b, chosen with the right-hand rule. Curl the fingers of your right hand from a towards b, and your thumb points along a × b.
  • Magnitude: |a × b| = |a| |b| sin θ.

Components

a × b = ⟨ a₂b₃ − a₃b₂,  a₃b₁ − a₁b₃,  a₁b₂ − a₂b₁ ⟩

Geometric meaning

The magnitude |a| |b| sin θ equals the area of the parallelogram with sides a and b. The height of that parallelogram is |b| sin θ, and the base is |a|.

Important properties

  • Order matters: b × a = −(a × b).
  • Parallel vectors give the zero vector, because sin 0 = 0.
  • The size is largest when the vectors are perpendicular (sin 90° = 1).
Key idea: the length of a cross product is the area of the parallelogram formed by the two vectors.

Try it

Adjust the two lengths and the angle until the area reaches 24. One solution is lengths 4 and 6 at a right angle (about 1.57 radians). Can you find a different one? Lengths 6 and 6 need an angle of roughly 0.73 radians, because sin(0.73) ≈ 0.67 and 36 × 0.67 ≈ 24.
