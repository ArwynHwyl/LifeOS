Vectors are not only about arrows. Their products measure real geometric quantities: the area of shapes and the volume of solids. Engineers use these calculations for surfaces, forces, and 3D graphics.

Areas from the cross product

  • Parallelogram with sides a and b: area = |a × b|.
  • Triangle with sides a and b: area = ½ |a × b|, exactly half the parallelogram.

Example

Let a = ⟨3, 0, 0⟩ and b = ⟨0, 4, 0⟩. Then a × b = ⟨0, 0, 12⟩ and its magnitude is 12. The parallelogram has area 12, and the triangle they form has area 6. That matches the rectangle area 3 × 4 = 12 that you can see by eye.

Volume from the triple product

Three vectors a, b, c that do not lie in one plane span a slanted box called a parallelepiped. Its volume is the absolute value of the scalar triple product:

V = | a · (b × c) |

The cross product b × c gives the area of the base. Dotting with a then multiplies by the height measured along the perpendicular direction.

  • If the triple product is zero, the three vectors lie in a single plane and the box is flat.
  • A tetrahedron built from the same three vectors has volume ⅙ |a · (b × c)|.
Key idea: cross product for area, triple product for volume. Both give sizes, so take the absolute value.
