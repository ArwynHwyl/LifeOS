Two different-looking conditions can behave identically. Recognising this lets you simplify code, prove that a refactor is safe, and choose the form that a person can read most easily.

Logical equivalence

Two expressions are logically equivalent, written with the symbol ≡, when they have the same truth value for every possible assignment of their variables. A truth table proves it: if the output columns match in every row, the expressions are equivalent.

Why care?

  • Refactoring: !(isEmpty || isHidden) may be clearer written another way, but only equivalent rewrites keep behaviour unchanged.
  • Performance and safety: a simpler condition means fewer checks and fewer places for bugs.
  • Tools: compilers and query planners apply these laws automatically.

The laws you will use

  • De Morgan: ¬(P ∧ Q) ≡ ¬P ∨ ¬Q and ¬(P ∨ Q) ≡ ¬P ∧ ¬Q
  • Double negation: ¬¬P ≡ P
  • Implication: P → Q ≡ ¬P ∨ Q
  • Distributive: P ∧ (Q ∨ R) ≡ (P ∧ Q) ∨ (P ∧ R)
  • Complement: P ∨ ¬P ≡ T and P ∧ ¬P ≡ F
  • Identity: P ∧ T ≡ P and P ∨ F ≡ P
Key idea: equivalent means the same output on every row. That is what makes a rewrite safe.
