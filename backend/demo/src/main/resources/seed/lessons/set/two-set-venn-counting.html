Venn diagrams are not only pictures. Attached to numbers they become a counting tool: how many students take maths or physics, how many users opened the app on web but not mobile, and so on.

Notation

  • n(A) is the number of members in set A.
  • n(U) is the size of the universal set: everything under discussion.

The overlap is counted twice

If you simply add n(A) and n(B), every member of the overlap is counted twice, once in each set. Subtract the overlap once to fix it:

n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)

The four regions

A only  = n(A) − n(A ∩ B)
B only  = n(B) − n(A ∩ B)
both    = n(A ∩ B)
outside = n(U) − n(A ∪ B)

Worked example

In a group of 30 students, 14 play football, 12 play basketball, and 5 play both. Then:

  • Football only: 14 − 5 = 9
  • Basketball only: 12 − 5 = 7
  • At least one sport: 14 + 12 − 5 = 21
  • Neither sport: 30 − 21 = 9

Check: 9 + 7 + 5 + 9 = 30, the whole group.

Key idea: the four regions always add up to the universal set. Use that as a sanity check.
