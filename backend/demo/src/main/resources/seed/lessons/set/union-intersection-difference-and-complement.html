Sets become useful when you combine them. Searching for “red or blue shoes”, “students who study maths and physics”, or “everything except spam” are all set operations in disguise.

The four basic operations

  • Union A ∪ B: everything that is in A, in B, or in both.
  • Intersection A ∩ B: only what is in both A and B.
  • Difference A − B: what is in A but not in B.
  • Complement A′: everything in the universal set U that is not in A.

A worked example

Let A = {1, 2, 3, 4} and B = {3, 4, 5}. Then:

A ∪ B = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}
A ∩ B = {3, 4}
A − B = {1, 2}
B − A = {5}

Notice that A − B and B − A are different sets: difference is not symmetric.

Reading a Venn diagram

A two-circle Venn diagram splits everything into four regions: A only, B only, both, and neither (outside both circles). In the diagram below the set totals are n(A) = 20 and n(B) = 15, and 6 members lie in the overlap. So A only = 20 − 6 = 14 and B only = 15 − 6 = 9, and the union has n(A ∪ B) = 20 + 15 − 6 = 29 members.

Key idea: the overlap belongs to both circles, so it must be counted once when you take a union.
