A Venn diagram separates the regions of sets A, B, and C visually before member counts are computed with inclusion-exclusion. The picture makes the algebra of the previous lesson feel obvious.

The seven regions

Three overlapping circles create seven regions inside them, plus one outside:

  • Three “only” regions: A only, B only, C only.
  • Three regions where exactly two circles overlap: A ∩ B only, A ∩ C only, B ∩ C only.
  • One region in the middle where all three overlap.
  • The area outside every circle.

Reading the diagram

Circle A is the whole disc: it contains its “only” region, both of its pairwise overlaps, and the centre. That is why n(A) includes members that also belong to B or C.

In this map, the example clubs have n(A) = 33, n(B) = 26, and n(C) = 22, with pairwise overlaps of 10, 8, and 7, and 3 members in all three.

Key idea: a whole circle is the sum of four regions: its “only” part, two pairwise parts, and the centre.
