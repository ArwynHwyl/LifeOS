The height and the width of a bell curve are controlled by different numbers. In this practice you will control the height of the standard bell shape and see exactly how the formula produces it.

The task

Adjust the scale a until the curve a · exp(−x²/2) passes through the target peak at (0, 1).

Reason it out

At the centre x = 0, the exponent is −0²/2 = 0, and exp(0) = 1. So the height at the centre is simply a × 1 = a. To reach a peak of 1 you need a = 1. The curve’s height at x = 0 is the scale.

What the scale changes

  • A larger a makes the whole curve taller.
  • The centre stays at x = 0, and the spread stays the same.

Why real densities have a smaller peak

A real normal density must enclose an area of exactly 1. To achieve this, the standard normal is multiplied by 1/√(2π) ≈ 0.399, which is why its true peak is about 0.4 rather than 1. Here we ignore that factor so the peak is easy to read on the axis.

Key idea: evaluate the formula at the point you care about, and the unknown often reveals itself.
